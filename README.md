# Pygmalion Project

**Stephen Gettel | Business 497 Senior Project | March 20, 2017**

This repository preserves a 2017 senior research project examining the Pygmalion effect in a professional workplace, with particular attention to trust between supervisors and employees, expectations, and perceived performance.

## About the study

The project combined a literature review with an anonymous, voluntary survey of **70 professionals at CH2M**. It examined how trust, expectations, perceived capability, and supervisor and respondent gender relate to self-reported workplace experiences. The report used Pearson correlations and reported P values from Minitab analyses.

The survey was conducted within one employer, with smaller subgroup counts. The original report acknowledges limitations and cautions against overgeneralization. These are historical findings, not evidence of causation or a newly verified statistical analysis.

## Preservation and future analysis

The 2017 report is reproduced for readability, **not rewritten or retrospectively corrected**. Its original claims, wording, survey questions, and reported statistics remain historical material. The original plain text version is preserved alongside it.

The associated Excel workbooks and Minitab projects are under review. Some raw exports include respondent identifiers and timestamps, so individual response records are **not being published here** pending a separate privacy and data integrity review. A reproducible modern reanalysis, if warranted, will be clearly labeled and kept separate from the original report.

## Research questions

The original paper considers how supervisor trust is built, how expectations relate to perceived performance, and whether respondent and supervisor gender are associated with differences in reported experiences. See the report for the full questions, methodology, findings, appendices, and references.
