# Research data and analysis archive

This folder documents the supporting materials for Stephen Gettel's 2017 Pygmalion Project. It is an archival guide, not a new statistical analysis.

## Historical materials reviewed

- Original report: 26-page Word document with the original survey instrument (Appendix A), 70-row coded response table (Appendix B), and selected reported P values (Appendix C).
- Scored Excel data: `Pygmalion Data Revised 2-15-2017.xlsx`, including the `Revised 2-2-17` worksheet containing 70 coded survey records and respondent/supervisor gender fields.
- Other revised workbooks dated February 2, 2017 and additional raw/scored survey exports; some versions contain respondent IDs and survey timestamps.
- Four historical Minitab `.mpj` projects with subgroup names. Their internal analysis outputs have **not** been independently extracted or validated.

The source files above were supplied for review but are **not included in this public folder**. Raw survey exports include identifiers and timestamps, and the several spreadsheet revisions have not yet been reconciled. Publication of respondent-level data should follow a privacy and integrity review.

## Survey structure

The scored response table contains 70 rows, Q1–Q4, Q5a–Q5f (multiple choice indicators), Q6–Q10, and respondent and supervisor gender codes (Q11–Q12). The report describes an anonymous voluntary workplace survey administered at CH2M, using self-reported ratings.

The 2017 report describes these subgroup sizes: female respondents with female supervisors (18), female respondents with male supervisors (24), male respondents with male supervisors (17), and male respondents with female supervisors (11). Total: 70.

## What the original report does and does not claim

The original study investigates associations between perceived supervisor trust, expectations, capability, and reported workplace performance. It reports Pearson correlations and selected P values. The study used one employer and small subgroup sizes. Correlation alone does not establish causation, and the subgroup findings should not be generalized without additional evidence.

## Future verification, separate from the 2017 paper

A later analysis could reconcile the Excel revisions against Appendix B, document the score coding and missing-value treatment, reproduce the original Pearson coefficients and P values, and assess the effect of multiple comparisons and ordinal survey responses. Any such analysis should be explicitly dated and labeled as a new evaluation. **No recalculation or verification is claimed here.**

## Source integrity

The original GitHub plain text transcription is retained without alteration. Its Appendix B heading does not include the embedded data table; the original Word report does. The formatted Markdown report therefore should not be treated as a complete reproduction of that embedded table.
